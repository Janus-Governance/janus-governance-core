RFC 0006 — Governance Evaluation Layer

Status: Draft
Author: Martín Nicolás Sánchez Morales
Created: 2026
Aligned with: Core Lite v0.2
Canonical specification: core/lite/JANUS_CORE_LITE_v0.2.md

Authority and scope

Core Lite v0.2 is the primary normative authority. This Draft describes evaluation without redefining Core. Other RFC Drafts and architecture documents are informative navigation, not constitutive authority.

Summary

Governance evaluation analyzes E+ and/or E− within a declared context. It may produce OMISSION_DETECTED when every Core condition is met. It does not produce, simulate, or originate HUMAN_DECISION.

Specification

Evaluation inputs

Evaluation may consider:

  • resolvably referenced immutable E+ artifacts;
  • E− only after every cumulative condition in Core §5 is satisfied;
  • applicable SCHEMA_LOG context;
  • relevant MANAGEMENT_LOG domain context; and
  • prior AUDIT_LOG history up to the declared cutoff.

Evaluation outputs

Evaluation may produce OMISSION_DETECTED when a verified omission satisfies the canonical contract. A deterministic reevaluation that only changes a derived view is not itself a canonical event.

Evaluation, recommendation, detection, escalation, and decision are distinct stages. When accountability requires judgment, evaluation may identify the need for human action or present evidence to a human authority. Only the human act may later be represented by HUMAN_DECISION; the flow does not originate that act.

Event recording

Canonical events are appended to AUDIT_LOG exclusively through an authorized Audit Writer. Evaluation does not write directly to AUDIT_LOG and does not modify MANAGEMENT_LOG or SCHEMA_LOG.

Informative architecture material

docs/architecture/governance-event-structure.md may illustrate an implementation-oriented event shape. It is informative and does not define the canonical contracts, which remain in Core Lite v0.2.

Semantic drift

An implementation may flag inconsistent terminology for audit attention. That flag is not E− unless the Core omission conditions are met, is not a new canonical event, and does not imply accountability. Human review may decide whether later action is authorized, without altering prior evidence or events.

Security considerations

Evaluation must not use incomplete scope or schema context, claim human authority, bypass the Audit Writer, mutate history, or treat a suite result as semantic conformity.
