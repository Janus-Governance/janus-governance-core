RFC 0003 — Omission Detection

Status: Draft
Author: Martín Nicolás Sánchez Morales
Created: 2026
Aligned with: Core Lite v0.2
Canonical specification: core/lite/JANUS_CORE_LITE_v0.2.md

Authority and scope

Core Lite v0.2 is the primary normative authority. This Draft explains omission detection without adding Core requirements. Any divergence is resolved in favor of the canonical Core and recorded rather than silently rewritten.

Summary

OMISSION_DETECTED records an instance of E− that satisfies every condition in Core Lite v0.2 §5. It records a verified omission; it is not a human decision and is not evidence that an implementation is semantically conformant.

Specification

Omission detection

A missing record alone is not E−. Omission detection requires, cumulatively:

  • a referenceable expectation version recorded before observation, including provenance, temporal applicability, and the due time or condition;
  • a deterministic scope containing sources, subject, filters, window, cutoff, and the versioned evaluation procedure;
  • a cutoff no earlier than the expectation's due time, with due status determinable from the consulted material;
  • applicable SCHEMA_LOG context for every record or event considered; and
  • a reproducible observation relative to the scope and cutoff.

OMISSION_DETECTED contract

When the cumulative conditions are met, evaluation may produce an OMISSION_DETECTED event. The event must include:

  • event_type;
  • event_id;
  • expectation_ref, identifying the expectation version evaluated;
  • scope_ref, identifying the deterministic search scope and cutoff;
  • schema_ref;
  • evidence_refs, containing at least one reference; and
  • recorded_at, the event's only own time.

management_refs is optional. Search execution time belongs in the observation artifact. The event asserts absence only within the recorded scope and up to the recorded cutoff. It is appended to AUDIT_LOG exclusively through an authorized Audit Writer.

Later evidence, correction, and resolution

Later evidence or corrective action does not overwrite, delete, or modify the historical event. Its effect appears in the deterministically rebuilt view and must be supported by referenceable evidence.

An omission is resolved only by a later evaluation of the same expectation version, using a later cutoff and corresponding evidence. Evaluation of a different expectation version does not resolve it. A later action satisfying the expectation is not a supersession of the historical event.

OMISSION_DETECTED is distinct from HUMAN_DECISION. Evaluation does not originate a human decision. A human decision about an omission modifies neither the event nor its evidence and constitutes neither E+ nor E−.

Security considerations

Incomplete search scope, retroactive expectations, inconsistent schemas, corrupted logs, unresolved references, or unauthorized writers must not be accepted as omission detection.
