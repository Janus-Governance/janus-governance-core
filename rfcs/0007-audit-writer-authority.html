RFC 0007 — Audit Writer Authority

Status: Draft
Author: Martín Nicolás Sánchez Morales
Created: 2026
Aligned with: Core Lite v0.2
Canonical specification: core/lite/JANUS_CORE_LITE_v0.2.md

Authority and scope

Core Lite v0.2 is the primary normative authority. This Draft explains the exclusive technical writer boundary. References to other Draft RFCs are informative and do not define the writer's authority.

Summary

An authorized Audit Writer is the exclusive mechanism for appending governance events to AUDIT_LOG. It records and validates events; it does not decide, originate human authority, or write to the other canonical logs.

Specification

Exclusive writer

All governance events appended to AUDIT_LOG must be written exclusively through an authorized Audit Writer. Direct writes that bypass it must be prevented.

The Audit Writer validates the applicable canonical event contract, stable identity, mandatory references, and append-only conditions before incorporation. A rejected write is a technical rejection; it is not a HUMAN_DECISION and does not create human authority.

Human decision boundary

For HUMAN_DECISION, the Audit Writer validates and incorporates a record of a prior human act. It must not originate, infer, simulate, or substitute for that act. The responsible authority is identified by actor_ref, not by the technical writer.

If mandatory object_refs do not resolve against already incorporated history, the Audit Writer prevents incorporation. It does not repair the decision by inventing objects or evidence.

Log boundary and append-only behavior

The Audit Writer appends only to AUDIT_LOG; it does not modify MANAGEMENT_LOG or SCHEMA_LOG. Corrections to domain activity are appended to MANAGEMENT_LOG; schema corrections or evolution are appended to SCHEMA_LOG; governance corrections are appended to AUDIT_LOG with a resolvable backward reference.

Prior events are never overwritten or deleted.

Security considerations

The authority boundary must be enforced at the storage or transport surface and must prevent bypass, event forgery, unresolved mandatory references, mutation of history, and autonomous creation of human authority.
