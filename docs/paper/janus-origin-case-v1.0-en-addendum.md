## Scope of the paper's thesis

In this paper, “deterministically reconstructible” denotes the proposed derivability of state from a chain of authorized decisions. This is a paper thesis and differs from Core Lite v0.2 rebuildability of governance-relevant views and findings. The proposed decision-chain condition applies as an optional framework profile to outcomes that require explicit human accountability; it is not a universal Core requirement.

The statement “Every datum must be bound to the decision that authorized it” is likewise the paper's central thesis and a proposed framework profile, not an invariant of Janus Core Lite.

## Evidence and canonical events

E+ is evidence explicitly recorded in a system of record. E- is evidence derived from a verified absence of an expected record or event. `OMISSION_DETECTED` records an instance of E- meeting Core Section 5; it is not E- itself. `HUMAN_DECISION` is required when an outcome requires explicit human accountability.

`janus_events.jsonl` is an implementation surface, not the Core ontology. A single physical file can host records only if it preserves the three conceptual canonical logs, their domain/governance separation, stable identities, and Audit Writer restrictions.

Figure 1 in v1.0 reverses the authorization flow by placing Decision after State. In the optional decision-binding profile, a required human decision precedes the accountable operation; the diagram does not impose that profile on every state transition.

Section VI-E describes an optional implementation profile. Its C1-C3 constraints add requirements beyond Core Lite. Corrections belong to the corresponding canonical log and reference their antecedent when they declare supersession, correction, or voiding. Log order alone is not normative authority.

## Nature of the case evidence

This paper presents a retrospective longitudinal practitioner study based on professional experience and corroborating publicly available sources. No confidential administrative records or identifiable personal data are reproduced.

The five-context, four-decade account differentiates publicly verifiable records, de-identified practitioner observations, retrospective practitioner recollection, and academic inference. “Longitudinal evidence” applies only where documentary support exists; otherwise the account is longitudinal practitioner observation or practitioner testimony.

The recurrence reported across the author's longitudinal professional experience suggests a persistent governance pattern and motivates a structural hypothesis; it does not, by itself, establish a universal invariant.

## External correspondences

NIST AI RMF 1.0 is intended for voluntary use. Janus records may support documented roles, responsibilities, and human-oversight processes; this does not establish NIST conformance.

Janus records can be mapped to PROV-DM only through an explicit profile. A decision may be represented as an activity associated with a responsible agent, optionally with a plan. PROV-DM has no native construct for the non-occurrence of an expected activity, so E- requires an extension.

No clause-level ISO/IEC 42001 claim is made. Such a mapping requires review of the licensed standard, and certification concerns an AI management system rather than a technical substrate alone.
